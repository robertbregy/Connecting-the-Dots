What you can investigate

A string is not the same as an application, and an application is not a delegation. Some names have multiple applicants, others never become active TLDs, and still others change operators or disappear from the DNS root. The Explorer follows these different states without counting them as the same thing.

  • Current root: inspect top-level domains in the authoritative IANA namespace.
  • Historical applications: follow the formal ICANN rounds of 2000, 2004 and 2012, including unsuccessful proposals.
  • 2026 Reveal Day: examine the locally preserved, dated primary-string snapshot, with evidence limits disclosed.
  • Context: explore disputes, contention, city and geographic names, economics, social meaning and unusual uses of domain endings.

Start with a question

Why does .io remain a country-code domain despite its global technology associations? What distinguishes brand namespaces from generics? What happens if several organisations seek the same string?

Understand the 2026 limitation

Research Release RR1 retains the official ICANN Reveal Day aggregate and a separately attributed, frozen secondary string inventory. It does not claim that all official 2026 APS application-level records have been imported locally.

Unknown outcomes remain unknown; historical presence is not proof of current delegation.

Sources and citation

Formal application data come from ICANN; current DNS-root evidence comes from IANA. Interpretations are labelled separately from documented facts.

Follow the Inside the Round 2026 chronology for dated ICANN milestones and a disclosed view through .lugano. Read the Research & citation overview, the working paper and the archived RR1 evidence release (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23262623).

## Rights, objections & remedies

Who can act, on what basis, who decides and what can happen next

A new gTLD application does not move from publication to delegation on data and evaluation alone. The 2026 Round also creates formal channels for the public, governments, rightsholders, communities and independent objectors to raise concerns. These mechanisms do not all have the same legal weight or effect.

**Not every intervention is an objection**
A public comment, a GAC Early Warning, GAC Consensus Advice, a singular/plural notification and a formal objection are different procedures. They involve different actors, decision-makers and possible consequences. Contention between applicants is different again.

Contention is competition between applications, not an objection. [Read contention →](https://robertbregy.github.io/Connecting-the-Dots/en/#contention)

## Seven ways an application can be challenged or influenced

The same application can be exposed to more than one channel. The question is not simply who objects, but which procedure they are using.

PROCEDURE

### Application comments

**Anyone**

ICANN and evaluation panels may consider them

No automatic effect on the application; relevant comments can inform evaluation.

PROCEDURE

### GAC Member Early Warning

**GAC members and observers**

No immediate decision

Signals a government concern. It does not by itself change application status.

PROCEDURE

### GAC Consensus Advice

**The GAC collectively**

ICANN Board

The Board decides whether the application may proceed, may not proceed, or may proceed with modifications.

PROCEDURE

### Singular / plural notification

**Anyone**

ICANN string evaluation

Triggers a singular/plural evaluation and may prevent an application from proceeding.

PROCEDURE

### Formal objection

**A party with standing**

Independent expert panel through the relevant DRSP

The panel applies one of four defined objection grounds and determines the application's ability to proceed.

PROCEDURE

### Independent Objector

**One of the three appointed Independent Objectors**

Independent expert panel

May bring Limited Public Interest or Community objections in the interests of global Internet users, not for a private client.

PROCEDURE

### Appeal

**The non-prevailing party to an objection**

The relevant dispute-resolution provider under the appeal procedure

A limited review of the panel determination under the clearly erroneous standard.

## The four formal objection grounds

A formal objection must fit one of four grounds and the objector must satisfy the standing rules for that ground. Filing is made directly with the designated Dispute Resolution Service Provider (DRSP).

WIPO

### String Confusion

Similarity in appearance, sound or meaning between the applied-for string or variants and another applied-for or existing TLD.

A successful objection can change contention or, for certain existing-TLD/ccTLD objectors, make an application ineligible to proceed.

WIPO

### Legal Rights

The applied-for string or an applied-for allocatable variant infringes existing legal rights, including relevant trademark rights.

A successful objection against the primary string makes the application ineligible to proceed.

ICC

### Limited Public Interest

The applied-for string is contrary to generally accepted legal norms of morality and public order recognized under principles of international law.

A successful objection against the primary string makes the application ineligible to proceed.

ICC

### Community

There is well-substantiated opposition from a significant portion of a clearly delineated community targeted explicitly or implicitly by the string.

A successful objection against the primary string makes the application ineligible to proceed.

## How a 2026 objection proceeds

This is the simplified procedural spine. Complex cases, consolidation, cooling-off periods or hearings can change the real timeline.

01

**17 Nov 2026**

String Confirmation Day opens the ordinary 104-day objection window.

02

**Filing**

The objector files electronically, in English, with the correct DRSP, identifies standing and the objection ground, supplies evidence and pays the applicable fee.

03

**Review**

The DRSP performs administrative review, appoints the panel and the panel conducts a Quick Look Review.

04

**Response**

After the required advance payments and transmission of the Quick Look result, the applicant has 30 days to respond.

05

**Determination**

The panel issues a reasoned determination. If the objection fails, the application may continue unless another process blocks it.

06

**Appeal**

The non-prevailing party has 15 days to notify an intent to appeal and a further 15 days to file the appeal and pay the required fees.

## What a decision can actually change

For String Confusion, the consequence can be a change to contention or, in defined cases involving existing TLD or ccTLD interests, ineligibility to proceed. For Legal Rights, Limited Public Interest and Community objections, a successful objection to the primary string makes the application ineligible to proceed. If only an allocatable variant is affected, the primary string and unaffected variants may still proceed. ICANN also describes cases in which a panel may require a new or modified Registry Voluntary Commitment (RVC), approved by ICANN, before an application can continue.

CONTRACT

## From dispute to binding commitment

An RVC can be proposed to address an objection or a government concern. If ICANN approves it and it is incorporated into the Registry Agreement, it becomes a binding contractual obligation. That is the bridge between a pre-delegation dispute and the rules under which a future registry may have to operate.

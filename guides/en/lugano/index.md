## Why would a city seek its own top-level domain?

The interesting question is not whether a city can place its name after a dot. It is what public value, safeguards and services could justify operating a distinct namespace. These are criteria for evaluation, not promises already fulfilled.

### A name people can recognise

A .lugano address could signal a connection with the city, but the suffix alone would not certify its owner. Any trustworthy use would require clear eligibility, verification and anti-impersonation rules.

### Public-interest governance

A civic namespace raises choices about reserved names, fair allocation, security, abuse handling and review of disputed decisions. These choices must be made transparent if the application succeeds; a domain extension does not settle them automatically.

### An address, not an identity certificate

The DNS translates names into technical destinations. It does not, by itself, prove that a service is official or trustworthy. Verification, accessible public registers and clear responsibility could add that layer.

### Build in stages, if approved

A possible sequence starts with a secure registry and transparent registration policy, then adds verified services and interoperable public-interest components. None of these stages is operational merely because the application is public.

## What Swiss precedents can and cannot tell us

Geographic and place-linked TLDs already exist. They help frame questions about eligibility, operation and public identity, but they do not predetermine the governance or the outcome of the .lugano application.

### Cities and territories have taken different paths

Cases such as .berlin, .nyc and .zuerich show that geographic names can enter the DNS under different operators and registration policies. A shared geographic label does not mean a shared governance model.

[Explore geographic TLDs](https://robertbregy.github.io/Connecting-the-Dots/en/?tab=explore&preset=cities#explore)

### .zuerich: a Swiss point of reference

The existing .zuerich Registry Agreement is a useful primary source for examining a Swiss geographic TLD. It is a precedent, not evidence that .lugano has been delegated or would use identical rules.

[ICANN · .zuerich ↗](https://www.icann.org/en/registry-agreements/details/zuerich?section=agreement)

**Distinctive value, not an invented first**
 Because .zuerich already exists, .lugano should not be presented as Switzerland's first geographic TLD. Its potential originality lies in the proposed public-interest use of naming, trust and digital commons, which would still need to be built and assessed.

## Turning a digital commons into verifiable choices

The .lugano case follows a project in formation. Official sources describe its intentions; the examples and operational criteria below are the author's analysis, not live services or adopted rules.

### What is documented

The City is the applicant and describes a staged opening with a substantial, verifiable territorial connection. Final criteria are not yet settled. The municipal FAQ says private sponsors cover the application fee, with no charge to city finances; that does not make future operating costs zero. lugano.ch remains the institutional website and MyLugano keeps its role.

### Who gets a name that represents a whole community?

Hypothetical examples, not allocations: cultura.lugano, ristoranti.lugano, quartieri.lugano. A collective name could be reserved, run as a shared gateway or allocated with explicit obligations, duration and transparent criteria. The proposal is to make eligibility, institutional-name protection, dispute handling and allocation review understandable, within ICANN rules and applicable law.

## .lugano — from a name to a trust layer

A vision for what the namespace could become if the application succeeds. This is direction, not an announcement of services already implemented.

The ambition is not simply to put Lugano after a dot. It is to create a public-interest namespace in which trust can become visible, governable and eventually machine-readable.

Public responsibilities, access criteria and review procedures should be identifiable in the published rules.

02 VISION

03 VISION

## Recognisable to people, verifiable by machines

 Analytical proposal, not adopted policy

The suffix alone certifies neither identity, quality nor authority. DNSSEC authenticates DNS data origin and integrity, not a registrant's commercial trustworthiness. For services and software agents, our proposal distinguishes who the entity is, which service it represents and which operations it may perform. Identification, verifiable service descriptions and limited, revocable authorisation need additional components.

A business admitted to .lugano does not become an official municipal service. Verified territorial membership must remain distinguishable from institutional representation, including for first-time visitors.

[IETF · RFC 4033, sections 3–4 ↗](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc4033)

## Open in response to usefulness, not just a calendar

 Analytical proposal, not adopted policy

### 1 · Recognisable early uses

Before scaling adoption, test a few useful services, clear responsibilities, support and user understanding.

### 2 · Broader local participation

Involve local organisations and registrars, explain criteria and prices, and measure actual use rather than name requests alone.

### 3 · Interoperable services

Trial additional functions only with verified identities, permissions, revocation and oversight. These are possibilities, not existing capabilities.

Before opening, test websites, email, forms, authentication and integrations: ICANN warns that delegation does not guarantee immediate software acceptance. Registrar distribution also needs to be built; it is not automatic.

[geoTLD.group · Local by Design (27.07.2026) ↗](https://geotld.group/new-geotld-advice-local-by-design/) [ICANN · Applicant Guidebook, 2.3 / 2.10 ↗](https://newgtldprogram-2026-agb.icann.org/en/6-module-2-general-information.html)

### Decisions still to be made

Who qualifies, which names are reserved, what prices and protections apply, how costs are funded and how outcomes are evaluated all need published answers when settled. We state no firm launch date and do not treat commercial expressions of interest as guaranteed reservations.

[Città di Lugano · progetto e FAQ (08.10.2026) ↗](https://www.lugano.ch/la-mia-citta/la-citta-si-racconta/progetti/dominio-primo-livello-lugano/) [ICANN · Pre-reserve a Domain Name, or Not? ↗](https://www.icann.org/en/blogs/details/pre-reserve-a-domain-name-or-not-icann-answers-the-question-13-11-2013-en)

## Risks and next checks

READING

## A vision to test against public evidence

These are editorial questions for assessing a possible civic namespace. They describe neither adopted municipal targets nor services already delivered.

### A clear use

Which task would become easier or more reliable for a person, organization or service? A distinctive suffix alone does not demonstrate that benefit.

### Actual adoption

Do names lead to services people use? Published examples of active use would be stronger evidence than the number of names reserved or announced.

### Rules that can be checked

Can users understand who is eligible, who is responsible and how a problem can be reported? Confidence should rest on published rules and accountable practice.

### Working connections

Can people and software use the services through documented interfaces and accepted standards? Public documentation and working demonstrations would make this claim testable.

## The economics of the dot

What is a dot worth?

TLDs are naming infrastructure, but they can also create public revenue, recurring registry income, scarcity rents, acquisition value and financial barriers to entry. These cases keep unlike metrics separate rather than forcing them into one league table.

Selection rule: each case illustrates a distinct economic mechanism and is supported by attributable financial or institutional evidence. Revenue, valuation, auction price and public receipts are not interchangeable measures.

From economic mechanisms to the existing market and the expectations behind the 2026 round.

## Where does the value come from?

Start with the mechanisms: not everyone invests in an extension for the same reason.

### Eight ways money enters the namespace

The same DNS root can generate very different economic relationships.

01

#### Sovereign digital rent

A country-code namespace becomes a material source of government revenue because global demand attaches value to its two-letter code.

02

#### National contract revenue

A state monetizes its ccTLD through a commercial registry or marketing agreement rather than operating a mass-market registrar business itself.

03

#### Operator acquisition

The company operating one or more namespaces is bought, turning expected registry cash flows into a transaction value.

04

#### Recurring registry economics

Large installed bases and annual renewals can create highly recurring infrastructure revenue, but company-level results must not be mislabeled as revenue from one TLD.

05

#### Valuation & regulation

A registry contract can underpin billion-dollar valuation while price controls, public-interest duties and change-of-control review shape the asset.

06

#### Scarcity & auction

When multiple applicants want the same string, scarcity can be converted into an auction price before the registry has sold a single domain.

07 2012 ROUND · HISTORICAL · PROHIBITED IN 2026

#### Private contention settlement (2012 · historical)

Historical 2012-round mechanism: applicants could receive consideration for withdrawing from contention, so abandoning a string could generate accounting gains. Private contention resolution, including settlement agreements and compensation for withdrawal, is prohibited in the 2026 round.

08

#### Cost of entry

Application fees and additional evaluation costs determine who can afford to compete for a place in the root.

**Scope and sources for each round**

#### 2000

47 applications received, 44 evaluated: US$2.2 million is reconstructed as 44 × US$50,000. Two applications had not paid and one was withdrawn over confidentiality. ICANN's February 2001 budget lists the same total as projected actual revenue; a separately verified final collection figure for the round is not established here.

[ICANN — New TLD Application Instructions, 15 August 2000, §§ I28–I29 ↗](https://archive.icann.org/en/tlds/new-tld-application-instructions-15aug00.htm) [ICANN — TLD Application Review Update, 23 October 2000 ↗](https://www.icann.org/en/announcements/details/tld-application-review-update-23-10-2000-en) [ICANN — FY2001–2002 Preliminary Budget, 19 February 2001, Schedule A ↗](https://www.icann.org/resources/unthemed-pages/preliminary-budget-2001-02-19-en)

#### 2004

Ten applications at US$45,000 each. ICANN explicitly reports US$450,000 collected in its 7 October 2004 report to the U.S. Department of Commerce.

[ICANN — Tenth Status Report to the U.S. Department of Commerce, 7 October 2004, p. 10 ↗](https://itp.cdn.icann.org/en/files/governance/mou-status-report-07oct04-en.pdf#page=10)

#### 2012

About US$357 million collected at launch, according to ICANN's program review. Its later FY12–FY23 statement reports about US$360 million in fees, US$52 million in refunds and US$308 million net: this covers a different period and is not the net of the initial US$357 million alone.

[ICANN — New gTLD Program Implementation Review, 29 January 2016, § 8.3.1; p. 183 ↗](https://www.icann.org/en/system/files/files/program-review-29jan16-en.pdf#page=183) [ICANN — FY26 Budget, May 2025, p. 29, § 5.1.1 ↗](https://www.icann.org/en/system/files/files/adopted-icann-budget-fy2026-published-2025-en.pdf#page=29)

#### 2026

1,615 is the official total published on 7 October; the public export analysed contains 1,614 applications plus replacement records. There are 51 ASP applications, with planned discounts of 75–85%, and waivers for some variants. Actual collections were not found in the sources reviewed: multiplying 1,615 × US$227,000 does not establish them.

[ICANN — 2026 Round gTLD Evaluation Fee FAQs ↗](https://newgtldprogram.icann.org/en/application-rounds/round2/2026-round-general/gtld-evaluation-fee/faqs) [ICANN — Reveal Day announcement, 7 October 2026 ↗](https://www.icann.org/resources/press-material/release-2026-10-07-en) [ICANN — 2026 Round APS statistics, published 7 October 2026 at 15:41 UTC ↗](https://newgtldprogram-aps.icann.org/statistics) [ICANN — 2026 Round Applicant Guidebook, V3, 7 October 2026, § 3.3 ↗](https://newgtldprogram.icann.org/sites/default/files/documents/new-gtld-program-2026-round-applicant-guidebook-current-en.pdf#page=66)

#### How to read the figures

Nominal amounts in U.S. dollars. The fee pays for application evaluation and does not guarantee a TLD. Documented totals and reconstructed figures are identified separately. Initial collections, refunds and multiyear statements cover different scopes; they are not profit. Conditional evaluation fees and annual registry fees are outside this comparison.

Auction proceeds are a separate category and are not included in application fees.

Valuation & regulation

Core economic question

#### Can a registry contract underpin a billion-dollar valuation?

FACT

## How do the bets change?

Expectations change over time. Economic cycles prepare the reading of applications without predicting their outcomes.

FACT READING

### Economic cycles: what applied-for strings reveal (and what they do not)

The dot-com bubble, platform expansion, crypto boom-and-bust cycles and current AI investment frenzy frame successive moments of demand for names. They are different waves, not one inevitable path from a popular term to a profitable registry.

#### The dot-com bubble: the web before the new rounds

The speculative surge and crash around 2000 occurred before the 2012 mass gTLD application round. Its legacy is a useful warning: a boom in companies, websites or domain prices does not prove that a new top-level registry will succeed.

#### Crypto: more than one market winter

Between the 2012 and 2026 rounds, crypto experienced repeated booms and contractions. A 2026 string application may signal renewed interest, but does not measure blockchain adoption, registry demand or an applicant's financial resilience.

#### AI: a different point in the cycle

Generative AI is still undergoing rapid commercialization. The editorial 2026 inventory includes AI-labelled applications, while the 2012 editorial comparison shows none in that category. Different taxonomies and the long gap require caution: names are not economic forecasts.

#### E-commerce, social networks and the physical economy

Platforms can create demand for recognisable identities without needing a TLD of their own. Conversely, banking, manufacturing, logistics, retail and public institutions may seek branded or sector-restricted namespaces as infrastructure rather than as speculative real estate.

Method: this is a historical interpretation of sectors and digital-market cycles, not proof of causation. Do not equate an application with delegation, actual registry operation, second-level registrations or financial success.

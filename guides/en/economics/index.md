# Who creates value, and who collects it?

Follow the money through the domain market, from registration payments to the strategies of extension operators.

[Explore in the atlas](https://robertbregy.github.io/Connecting-the-Dots/en/?tab=economics#economics) [Method and citation](https://robertbregy.github.io/Connecting-the-Dots/research/) [Text version](https://robertbregy.github.io/Connecting-the-Dots/guides/en/economics/index.md)

The point, in brief

Registrations, active websites and applications measure different things. Before comparing figures, check the unit, period and source.

A name can generate recurring revenue, contribute to a territory’s public finances or attract investment before it is used. Its meaning matters, alongside the rules governing its allocation and the organisation that operates it.

This introduction connects the main mechanisms. The detailed chapters contain the rankings, all eight cases, applicants’ strategies and analysis of the 2026 round, with data and sources to consult. Each chapter can be read on its own.

## Who pays whom?

The price paid by someone registering a domain belongs to a wider chain. For generic domains, relationships with ICANN form part of the contractual framework; country-code domains may follow different arrangements.

- ### The registrant registers the name

A person or organisation chooses a name and pays for registration or renewal under the service’s terms. This is where the domain becomes part of everyday use.

- ### The registrar provides the service

The registrar provides the public registration channel and passes operations to the registry. The price offered to the customer and the fee charged by the registry belong to different levels.

- ### The registry operates the extension

The registry operator maintains the TLD’s database and sets its commercial model and policies within the applicable framework. Registrations and renewals can support a recurring business.

Public revenue, a company’s results and auction prices show three different ways in which a name can generate value or be assigned a value.

### A name in a territory’s finances

.ai is Anguilla’s country-code domain. Its association with artificial intelligence has given these two letters a global commercial meaning. The case follows public revenue from registrations, distinguishing actual amounts from projections and budget estimates. It provides a way to examine the relationship between demand for names and a territory’s public finances.

The .web case, alongside .shop and .app, shows the significance of competition for the same string. An auction price concerns that procedure and the applicants’ expectations. ## Before comparing two figures

Check what they measure, which period they refer to and which source documents them. Revenue, an acquisition price and a budget forecast answer different questions. The currency and the scope of the organisation also matter.

Registrations describe a base of names; presence on websites observes a different kind of use. Applications describe intentions and strategies. None of these measures, taken alone, is enough to demonstrate an extension’s future profitability.

## Value has a context

Domain economics takes shape where infrastructure, rules and meanings meet. The useful question is which mechanism produces value, for whom, and what evidence allows us to identify it.

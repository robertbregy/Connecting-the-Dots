## The archaeology of the dot

Follow what was imagined, what was formally applied for, what reached the root, and how names later moved, changed operator, were retired or became disputed.

1984

 PROTOCOL

**Before .com, there was .arpa** RFC 920 described .arpa as the initial, transitional top-level domain for the ARPA-Internet while the DNS was being introduced. It was supposed to disappear; instead it survived and today serves Internet infrastructure.

[Source 1 ↗](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc920/) [Source 2 ↗](https://www.iana.org/domains/arpa)

1985

 ROOT MILESTONE

**The first .com: symbolics.com** In January 1985, .com, .edu, .gov, .mil, .net and .org joined .arpa in the root. On 15 March, symbolics.com became the first registered .com domain.

[Source 1 ↗](https://www.iana.org/reports/2001/name-report-16aug01.html) [Source 2 ↗](https://www.icann.org/en/blogs/details/celebrating-the-rise-of-the-modern-internet-the-first-dot-com-domain-name-turns-30-16-3-2015-en)

1999

 IDEA

**The city idea appears** The .berlin concept is traced back to 1999.

[Source ↗](https://newgtlds.icann.org/en/announcements-and-media/case-studies/berlin-a4-13feb19-en.pdf)

 APPLICATION ROUND

**Proof of concept** 47 applications. Even .nyc appears, then withdraws.

[Source ↗](https://archive.icann.org/en/tlds/app-index.htm)

2004

 APPLICATION ROUND

**Sponsored communities** 10 applications for 9 strings: .asia, .cat, .jobs, .mail, .mobi, .post, .tel, .travel and .xxx.

[Source ↗](https://www.icann.org/en/announcements/details/icann-progress-in-process-for-introducing-new-sponsored-top-level-domains-19-3-2004-en)

 APPLICATION ROUND

 DELEGATION / LAUNCH

**Cities go live** .berlin opens to the public; .nyc and .paris are delegated.

**Reading method and limits**

This is exploratory classification; independent validation has not been completed. Applied-for words document proposals, not by themselves a change in society as a whole. Comparing eras requires consistent rules, label review and evidence about actual use.

Beyond the dot lie institutions, markets, communities and ideas about the future. Reading TLDs over time helps us follow changes in the Internet and the ways people seek a place within it. This historical interpretation draws on documented cases: categories overlap, and existing names can acquire new meanings.

1984–1985 · defining the original categories

### Before the Web: giving the network an order

**Documented fact.** In October 1984, RFC 920 describes .com for commercial activities, .edu for education, .gov for government, .mil for the military and .org for other organizations, alongside country codes. The Web would only be proposed in 1989: .com predates company websites.

**Interpretation.** The earliest naming map mainly reflects the institutions and administrative responsibilities of a network that predates the Web. Country codes place geography alongside this classification by function.

[IETF · RFC 920, Domain Requirements (1984) ↗](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc920/) [CERN · The birth of the Web ↗](https://home.cern/science/computing/the-birth-of-the-web)

1993–1994 · opening the Web and the growth of .com

### An address becomes a public presence

**Documented fact.** On 30 April 1993, CERN releases the Web software into the public domain. By March 1994, RFC 1591 already describes .com as a much enlarged category intended for companies. In the same document, .org remains the category for organizations that do not fit elsewhere.

**Interpretation.** As the Web spreads, an address can become a shop window, a service and a public identity. Reading .com as a symbol of commercial presence belongs to this later history of use, rather than its technical origins. A suffix's original purpose does not exhaust its meaning.

[CERN · The birth of the Web ↗](https://home.cern/science/computing/the-birth-of-the-web) [IETF · RFC 1591, Domain Name System Structure and Delegation (1994) ↗](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1591.html)

2000 and 2004 rounds · later introductions; .mobi revised in 2017

### People, communities and ways to connect

**Documented fact.** In 2000, ICANN selects seven new proposals, including .name and .coop. The 2003–2004 process brings .cat for the Catalan linguistic and cultural community and .mobi for mobile access. In 2017, ICANN approves .mobi's conversion to a standard gTLD without retaining its mobile charter.

**Interpretation.** Names begin to declare who we are, where we belong and how we connect. .mobi also preserves an expectation later recast in its rules: a documented transformation that does not, by itself, establish commercial failure.

[ICANN · New TLDs: FAQs (17 November 2000) ↗](https://archive.icann.org/en/tlds/new-tld-faq.htm) [IANA · Report on the Delegation of .CAT (2005) ↗](https://www.iana.org/reports/2005/cat-report-18nov2005.html) [ICANN · .MOBI Sponsorship Charter (2005) ↗](https://www.icann.org/es/registry-agreements/mobi/mobi-appendix-s-23-11-2005-en) [ICANN · Board minutes, resolution 2017.03.16.04 ↗](https://www.icann.org/en/board-activities-and-meetings/materials/minutes-regular-meeting-of-the-icann-board-16-03-2017-en)

May 2010 · first internationalized ccTLD delegations

### An address can end in our own script

**Documented fact.** On 5 May 2010, ICANN announces the first internationalized ccTLDs delegated into the root: Arabic names for Egypt, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates. Requests had opened in November 2009. These are not TLDs from the 2012 round; they follow the dedicated country-code process.

**Interpretation.** Belonging is expressed through more than territory or activity. It can also appear in the script a person reads and uses to communicate. This is a concrete step toward a more linguistically plural network, although technical availability alone does not measure effective inclusion.

[ICANN · First IDN ccTLDs Available (5 May 2010) ↗](https://www.icann.org/en/announcements/details/first-idn-cctlds-available-5-5-2010-en)

2012 round · delegations from 2013; launches and use in different years

### Cities and brands enter the same space

**Documented fact.** The 2012 round receives 1,930 applications, including geographic proposals and names in other scripts. That same round brings .berlin, publicly available in March 2014, and .google, restricted to Google and its affiliates. .google is delegated in September 2014; Google dates its first use to 2016.

**Interpretation.** The map expands: urban identities and control of a brand coexist within one expansion. These are overlapping projects enabled by the same rules, not eras replacing one another. The gap between application, delegation and use reveals the time needed to turn a name into a presence.

[ICANN · New gTLD Reveal Day (13 June 2012) ↗](https://www.icann.org/en/announcements/details/new-gtld-reveal-day---applied-for-strings-13-6-2012-en) [ICANN · Delegated Strings, 2012 round ↗](https://newgtlds.icann.org/en/program-status/delegated-strings) [ICANN / dotBERLIN · .BERLIN case study ↗](https://newgtlds.icann.org/sites/default/files/berlin-a4-13feb19-en.pdf) [Google Registry · .google Domain Registration Policy ↗](https://www.registry.google/policies/registration/google/) [Google Registry · 3 lessons learned from launching .google (2025) ↗](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/registry/3-lessons-learned-from-launching-google/)

2012 round · .bank agreement in 2014; current requirements consulted in 2026

### A name can promise verified membership

**Documented fact.** The .bank agreement sets banking-community rules and eligibility verification. The current registry restricts registration, including regulated banks and certain related organizations.

**Interpretation.** A suffix can set an access boundary as well as name an activity. Trust is a project supported by rules here: these sources neither measure users’ trust nor establish an absence of abuse.

[ICANN · .bank Registry Agreement, Specification 12 (2014) ↗](https://itp.cdn.icann.org/en/files/registry-agreements/bank/bank-agmt-html-25sep14-en.htm) [fTLD · .Bank registrant eligibility ↗](https://register.bank/eligibility/)

2012 round · .blog opens in 2016; WordPress.com subdomains in 2018

### Publishing a voice, within a platform

**Documented fact.** Nominet announces .blog general availability on 21 November 2016. In November 2018, WordPress.com offers free addresses beneath names such as food.blog: subdomains, rather than independent .blog domain registrations.

**Interpretation.** Web participation gains a recognizable name while access can remain mediated by a platform. A personal address does not imply infrastructure control: editorial openness and technical dependence can coexist.

[Nominet · .blog general availability (21 November 2016) ↗](https://nominet.uk/news/blog-enters-general-availability-phase/) [WordPress.com · Free .blog subdomains (28 November 2018; updated 2024) ↗](https://wordpress.com/blog/2018/11/28/announcing-free-dotblog-subdomains/)

Current reuse of .ai · 2026 applications published on 7 October

### Changing times give existing names new meanings

**Documented fact.** .ai remains Anguilla's country code, while its official registry also promotes it as a sign of innovation and artificial intelligence. On 7 October 2026, ICANN publishes applications for the new round: proposals still under review, rather than newly operational TLDs.

**Interpretation.** A name can express the present without being newly created. .ai shows a new cultural association attached to existing infrastructure; the 2026 applications reveal what participants would like to name next. Origin, aspiration and use are three traces to read together.

[IANA · .ai, country-code TLD for Anguilla ↗](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/ai.html) [.ai · Official Registry Operator Website ↗](https://www.nic.ai/) [ICANN · Applications revealed, 2026 round (7 October 2026) ↗](https://www.icann.org/en/announcements/details/icann-reveals-2026-round-applications-for-new-generic-top-level-domains-07-10-2026-en)

TLDs are not a complete mirror of society. They show who was able to request a name, what space they obtained and how that name was used. The most revealing trajectory emerges where these three histories meet.

**How to read this trajectory**

Each stage separates documented facts from interpretation; the selected examples are not a census. The reproducible census below includes every string from the sources collected for this project and applies the same present-day classification vocabulary across the different rounds. Semantic assignment is incomplete: names without a category remain in the corpus, and counts depend on rule coverage. This reading does not automatically reconstruct each name's historical meaning or represent the full range of online identities. Application, delegation and public availability are distinct events; the counts do not measure use or adoption.

**Method and limits**

Counts use distinct strings, not applications: competing applicants do not increase a name’s weight. Categories may overlap. Themes describe the string, not the applicant’s industry or social impact. Official 2026 brand, community and geographic fields are not projected backwards onto previous rounds. Replacement records and variant-only applications are retained in the census but excluded from the main 2026 comparison. Missing classification is not evidence that a phenomenon is absent.

Dated local corpora, not a universal list of every name ever proposed. The 2026 applications express intentions and do not establish future delegation or use.

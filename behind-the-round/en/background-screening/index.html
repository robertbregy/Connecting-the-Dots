FIELD NOTE 05 · 17 OCTOBER 2026

Who may operate a TLD, and where will auction proceeds go?

On 15 October ICANN named its applicant-screening provider. Two days earlier the Board chair previewed a discussion on potential auction proceeds. These are distinct questions, but they show that allocating a TLD involves more than assigning a name.

Field note index Back to the process timeline

Announcements confirmed · no individual screening outcomes or auction decisions

15 October: the screening provider is selected

ICANN announced an agreement with PricewaterhouseCoopers Advisory Services LLC (PwC) to carry out background screening for the 2026 round. A competitive procurement process began in November 2025. This is a procedural milestone, not an announcement that any individual application has been approved, rejected or screened.

What is checked, and who is checked

Background screening is mandatory during applicant evaluation. ICANN refers to organisations, individuals named in corporate account records, public information and Applicant Guidebook eligibility criteria: corporate standing, specified convictions or regulatory actions, and repeated cybersquatting. These criteria have defined thresholds. The existence of screening is not evidence of wrongdoing by an applicant.

Different checks, no manufactured causal link

Selecting a provider, conducting future background checks and performing an administrative check on a particular application are distinct actions. Sanctions regimes have separate legal bases. None of the official sources reviewed here links PwC's appointment to the administrative check on .wdo or to the gap between 1,616 paid applications and the 1,615 reported on Reveal Day. Coincidence is not an explanation.

13 October: the question of potential auction value

ICANN Board chair Tripti Sinha said the Board would begin discussing how to define uses of any potential 2026-round auction proceeds. No allocation or amount was approved in that statement. Separate from PwC's screening remit, this raises a public-interest question about a shared scarce resource: beyond who receives a contested string, what happens to any economic value produced by its allocation?

Evidence to watch, rather than assume

We will follow ICANN's published evaluation developments and actual Board decisions on auction proceeds. Appointing a service provider is not a screening outcome, and a planned discussion is not a resolution. These announcements establish no specific finding about .wdo, .lugano or any other applicant.

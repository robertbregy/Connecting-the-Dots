Field Note 04 · Counting

1,169 replacement strings: the extra rows behind Reveal Day

A CSV can have more rows than applications. Alternative strings, early deactivation labels and variant-only records explain several numbers that look contradictory when read without their definitions.

Source transparency: internet.Best is itself a 2026 applicant for .socialmedia. Its figures are treated as secondary evidence, not as an ICANN-certified reconciliation.

2,783 rows do not mean 2,783 applications

Stéphane Bortzmeyer's extraction counted 2,783 CSV records, but 1,614 application records after excluding replacement strings. The difference of 1,169 corresponds to the alternatives recorded by internet.Best. APS explicitly explains that a primary record and its replacement can appear separately while counting as one application in the official statistics. The official 1,615 figure remains a distinct source and is not silently replaced by 1,614.

727 deactivated alternatives: what the label does not prove

In its Reveal Day reconstruction, internet.Best marks 442 replacement rows active and 727 deactivated. The figures describe alternative records, not 727 rejected main applications. ICANN defines 'Deactivated' as a final application-record status but says that non-selected replacement strings are deactivated after String Confirmation Day. The historical secondary labels therefore need row-level and timestamp checks before we attribute reasons or dates to every record.

980 primary strings and a secondary list of 986

The project's frozen nTLDData string inventory contains 980 distinct primary strings associated with 1,608 applications. Six additional secondary-index applications are variant-only applications for existing TLDs, outside that primary-string inventory. internet.Best reports 986 distinct strings under its own extraction. The numerical match 980 + 6 = 986 is suggestive but not, by itself, a verified one-to-one reconciliation of string identities; that requires checking individual application IDs.

The other gap: 47 submitted applications

ICANN reported 1,663 submitted applications after the 12 August deadline and 1,616 paid applications proceeding on 22 September. Thus 47 submissions are outside the later paid-and-proceeding total. This is a change in eligibility and accounting stage, not evidence of 47 formal rejections. It also does not answer why the published figure shifted again from 1,616 to 1,615 on Reveal Day.

An irreversible choice before 21 October

The replacement window runs 8–21 October 2026, with string confirmation scheduled for 17 November. Eligible applicants may elect an alternative only once; they cannot reverse that choice. An alternative identical to another proposed primary or replacement string is ineligible, and the Guidebook restricts coordination among competing applicants. Final outcomes must not be inferred from Reveal Day status labels.

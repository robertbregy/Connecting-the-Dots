Field Note 03 · Procedure

When the safeguards' own recruitment system failed

An email inbox refused outside messages, and ICANN says a submission portal closed 24 hours before its deadline. These are not rumours about gTLD applications: ICANN documented the two technical problems in the Independent Objector selection process.

Field note index Back to the process timeline

Documented procedural incidents · individual effects not quantified

An inbox that could not receive

On 30 June 2026 ICANN reported that the dedicated email inbox for Expressions of Interest from potential Independent Objectors had been configured to reject messages from external senders. The defect was discovered following a respondent's report. ICANN fixed the configuration but said the earlier messages could not be recovered.

The portal closed 24 hours early

ICANN reports that the EOI portal inadvertently closed on 30 May 2026, describing this as 24 hours before the deadline. The same notice identifies 1 June as the previously extended deadline. Without precise closing times and time zones, the calendar dates and stated 24-hour interval cannot be independently reconciled. Several parties were prevented from submitting; the technical cause remained unidentified in the 30 June report.

Corrective actions and unanswered questions

ICANN notified interested parties, offered an alternative submission channel and later kept the EOI open until all Independent Objector roles were filled. The public record does not establish how many emails were lost, whether anyone ultimately failed to participate or the cause of the early portal shutdown.

Why this is relevant to the 2026 round

Independent Objectors may raise Community or Limited Public Interest objections without representing a private applicant. Failures in their recruitment process therefore matter for procedural accessibility and trust. They do not prove a fault in TAMS, an altered Reveal Day application count, or misconduct in the evaluation of TLD applications.

CASE 01 · REVEAL DAY 2026

One application missing. Two different questions.

The figures differed by just one, yet the distinction matters. A paid-application count, an official Reveal Day total and a frozen secondary index do not describe exactly the same publication state.

Partly explained · one question remains open
1,616ICANN, 22 September · paid applications proceeding
1,615ICANN, 7 October · official Reveal Day applications
1,614Secondary snapshot of 7 October · observed on 8 October

The 1,614 figure comes from the frozen nTLDData-based index (480 applicants), not an ICANN-published application-level export.

Documented omission 1,615 → 1,614

The footnote that resolves the public-list discrepancy

ICANN's APS Applications page explicitly says that application WDO2627T-T45217 for .wdo (World Data Organization, China) is still undergoing an administrative check and is therefore absent from the public applications list. The application detail page independently confirms that status. This gives a documented explanation for why the visible list can contain one fewer application than the official aggregate. The independent secondary snapshot is consistent with that omission; its coverage is not being certified as a complete APS export.

Cause not established 1,616 → 1,615

Why did the official count fall before Reveal Day?

On 22 September ICANN reported 1,616 paid applications proceeding. On 7 October the official Reveal Day total was 1,615. The verified public sources reviewed here do not identify which record accounts for the change or why. Withdrawal, administrative rejection, a payment issue or a timing adjustment would be speculation. This question remains open.

The regional discrepancy: a second check

Only Asia-Pacific differs between the official Reveal Day application counts and the project's preserved secondary index. The applicant count also differs by one in precisely that region.

Across five regions, official totals add to 1,615 applications from 481 applicants; the secondary index has 1,614 from 480. The .wdo applicant is in China. The regional match supports the APS footnote but does not replace a one-to-one reconciliation of all application IDs.

An independent researcher's CSV check

On 8 October, Stéphane Bortzmeyer examined the public CSV: 2,783 total rows, 1,614 applications when replacement-string rows are removed, and 480 applicants. Exported rows, applications and requested strings are not interchangeable because one application can yield several rows. The independent check corroborates the visible shortfall but does not explain the earlier change in the official total.

A widely reported explanation is not a complete reconciliation

CircleID placed the .wdo administrative check alongside the change from 1,616 to 1,615. In its 8 October notice JPNIC similarly described 1,616 paid applications, 1,615 published and one under administrative check. Yet APS already reports 1,615 active in its statistics while the independent public index identifies 1,614. These accounts are relevant interpretations, not an ID-by-ID reconciliation proving which application caused the earlier official decrease.

Why should anyone care about a difference of one?

Because a statistic, an application record and a publishable application list are different objects. The exercise shows why researchers must capture the date, scope and source of a number before comparing it, and why public visibility should never be confused with acceptance, approval or eventual delegation.

Evidence, not speculation

The 1,616 and 1,615 totals are direct ICANN statements; 1,614 is a time-stamped secondary index preserved by this project. The APS footnote documents .wdo's omission, but does not explain the earlier change in ICANN totals. The two gaps are therefore tracked separately. This episode is a corrigible editorial observation, not a revision of the archived RR1 dataset.

What would close the remaining question?

A dated ICANN explanation connecting the September and October totals, a change to the .wdo administrative-check record, or a public list that can be reconciled application by application. Any substantive correction will be dated, sourced and documented in the project history.

CASE 02 · NAMES AND PRIOR RIGHTS

Two organizations, one acronym: who may seek .wdo?

The .wdo applicant is the World Data Organization, launched in Beijing in 2026. But the World Design Organization dates back to 1957, uses WDO and holds a registered trademark. Is there already a legal dispute? Public evidence does not establish one.

Shared acronym documented · No verified dispute
World Data OrganizationBeijing · established 2026 · .wdo applicant
World Design OrganizationMontréal · founded 1957 · wdo.org

First distinction: they are not the same organization

ICANN names World Data Organization, located in China, as the applicant for .wdo under WDO2627T-T45217, still undergoing an administrative check. World Design Organization is a separate Montréal-based body involved in industrial design and using wdo.org. Sharing the initials WDO proves no corporate or institutional connection between them.

Two histories, almost seventy years apart

World Data Organization was launched in Beijing on 30 March 2026; the Chinese Academy of Sciences reports that Tan Tieniu was elected council chair. World Design Organization was founded in 1957 as Icsid and adopted its current name in 2015. The application therefore concerns initials with an established public history, but that history alone cannot determine entitlement to the TLD.

A verifiable earlier right is not an automatic veto

Canada's CIPO registry records the word mark WORLD DESIGN ORGANIZATION (WDO), filed on 23 October 2015 and registered as TMA1008130 on 2 November 2018, with active registration. Trademark protection has a defined scope: it does not automatically confer ownership of the .wdo top-level domain, nor prove that the organization has filed an objection.

What does the ICANN procedure allow?

The 2026 Applicant Guidebook permits a Legal Rights Objection by a party with standing claiming infringement of existing rights, backed by evidence. ICANN announced a public-input and objections window running from 17 November 2026 to 16 March 2027. Possible standing is a starting point, not an adjudicated claim. As of 10 October, no objection from World Design Organization concerning .wdo has been verified.

Compliance context, not an explanation for the check

Another documented fact concerns Tan Tieniu: the Chinese Academy of Sciences identifies him as a World Data Organization leader, while a 17 July 2026 US OFAC update lists him under Hong Kong Autonomy Act measures on the Non-SDN Menu-Based Sanctions List. That entry concerns an individual, not proof that World Data Organization is itself designated. No ICANN source connects it to the administrative check of .wdo; a causal claim would be speculation.

What remains unknown

ICANN has not published the reason for the administrative check on WDO2627T-T45217. There is no verified objection by World Design Organization, refusal, established infringement, or decision on the merits of the application. Neither the new organization's age, another body's trademark nor a leader's sanctions record can be used to infer the cause.

How this case will be followed

A revision will require new primary evidence: an APS status change, published comments or objections involving .wdo, official statements by either WDO, a decision affecting the name, or a relevant OFAC change. Each change will be dated and sourced. Research must not invent a dispute before one exists.

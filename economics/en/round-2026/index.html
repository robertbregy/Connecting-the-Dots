About US$357 million collected at launch, according to ICANN's program review. Its later FY12–FY23 statement reports about US$360 million in fees, US$52 million in refunds and US$308 million net: this covers a different period and is not the net of the initial US$357 million alone.

How to read the figures

Nominal amounts in U.S. dollars. The fee pays for application evaluation and does not guarantee a TLD. Documented totals and reconstructed figures are identified separately. Initial collections, refunds and multiyear statements cover different scopes; they are not profit. Conditional evaluation fees and annual registry fees are outside this comparison.

Auction proceeds are a separate category and are not included in application fees.

Editorial review · 19 October 2026

How do the bets change?

Expectations change over time. Economic cycles prepare the reading of applications without predicting their outcomes.

FACTREADING

Economic cycles: what applied-for strings reveal (and what they do not)

The dot-com bubble, platform expansion, crypto boom-and-bust cycles and current AI investment frenzy frame successive moments of demand for names. They are different waves, not one inevitable path from a popular term to a profitable registry.

The dot-com bubble: the web before the new rounds

The speculative surge and crash around 2000 occurred before the 2012 mass gTLD application round. Its legacy is a useful warning: a boom in companies, websites or domain prices does not prove that a new top-level registry will succeed.

Crypto: more than one market winter

Between the 2012 and 2026 rounds, crypto experienced repeated booms and contractions. A 2026 string application may signal renewed interest, but does not measure blockchain adoption, registry demand or an applicant's financial resilience.

AI: a different point in the cycle

Generative AI is still undergoing rapid commercialization. The editorial 2026 inventory includes AI-labelled applications, while the 2012 editorial comparison shows none in that category. Different taxonomies and the long gap require caution: names are not economic forecasts.

E-commerce, social networks and the physical economy

Platforms can create demand for recognisable identities without needing a TLD of their own. Conversely, banking, manufacturing, logistics, retail and public institutions may seek branded or sector-restricted namespaces as infrastructure rather than as speculative real estate.

Method: this is a historical interpretation of sectors and digital-market cycles, not proof of causation. Do not equate an application with delegation, actual registry operation, second-level registrations or financial success.

Method, coverage and sources

The 891 synthetic records of the earlier reconstruction are replaced in the current layer by applications with official identifiers. All 1,608 comparable rows link individually to APS. Ultimate ownership is not inferred from an applicant’s name. This is a new current analysis: it is not contained in the immutable RR1/RR2 deposits and does not inherit their DOI. The scope uses original Reveal Day primary strings, not the strings that will ultimately be confirmed.

Brands, products and AI agent identities: different logics

ICANN reports 333 brand applications on Reveal Day. A corporate brand, a product name and a possible digital-agent identity are different strategies. Using TLDs as trust infrastructure for AI agents remains a hypothesis, not an established result.

AI and crypto appear in the 2026 subset

The 2026 subset contains records classified under AI and crypto; the equivalent editorial categories have no records in 2012. This describes the vocabulary and classification of these subsets. It does not mean those technologies did not exist in 2012, or establish future adoption.

Finance: institutions and generic terms

The finance category combines identified organisations with generic financial terms. Corporate or brand names and references to a financial market express different naming strategies; their combined share does not count the financial institutions participating in the round.

Media and communications: a smaller share of the subset

In the historical classification, media and communications account for 13.7% of the 2012 subset and 8.0% of the 2026 subset. The category has a smaller relative share; this does not establish a decline in those industries or in their economic value.

Identity and digital trust: a larger share of the subset

The cybersecurity, identity and trust category accounts for 0.9% of the 2012 classified subset and 5.4% of the 2026 subset. That is a larger share in this map, not a measure of growth in the industry. Any future practical value of the names would depend on governance, services and actual use.

Financial names: brands and shared terms

A bank's own TLD and a term such as .bank describe very different allocation models. A string's sector does not establish who may register, who governs it, or whether it will be delegated.

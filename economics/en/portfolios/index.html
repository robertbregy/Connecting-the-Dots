Who applies at scale?

A TLD round is not only a collection of individual ideas. Some applicants seek dozens or hundreds of strings, turning naming into a portfolio strategy. Compare the legal applicant, the unit counted and the provenance before drawing conclusions.

Method

Aruba: why 39 and 20 can both be correct

Aruba announced 39 applied-for strings including replacements; the frozen secondary index lists 20 live primary applications for Aruba PEC S.p.A. These are different units and scopes. Adding or comparing them as equivalent counts would be misleading.

Who controls a name: the applicant, the corporate group or the eventual registry operator?
What the ranking really reveals
Large application portfolios reveal a strategy, not a guaranteed market position. Applications can fail, enter contention, be withdrawn or remain undelegated. Concentration at application stage is not identical to concentration of functioning TLD registries.

The 2012 and 2026 figures are not a directly comparable corporate-group ranking. The 2026 counts use 1,614 published applications, including six existing-TLD variant applications; the official reported total is 1,615. They describe the captured applications and their original primary labels, not final confirmed strings or awarded TLDs.

Words are a market. Not yet an outcome.

In the 2026 round, competition for identical strings reveals applicants' economic expectations. Filing does not mean winning a TLD, and contention is not a direct measure of future demand.

Scarcity forms around a word

Our analysis of the 7 October APS snapshot finds 263 exact-primary-string groups comprising 891 applications. These are analytical groups, not ICANN's definitive contention sets. Replacements and subsequent evaluations can change the picture.

Intercap: applying as a portfolio strategy

Domain Name Wire (7 October) counts 71 Intercap applications: 58 strings in contention and 13 uncontested. Our comparison of original primary strings in the 11 October APS snapshot gives 54 with exact matches and 17 without: a derived, unofficial count. This is a portfolio strategy, not completed acquisitions.

Contention and portfolios: additional 2026 evidence
FACTREADING

The competition hidden inside 980 strings

The 263 preliminary identical-string contention sets contain 891 applications. That is 55.2% of the secondary index's 1,614 applications, although the 263 contested strings are only 26.8% of the 980 primary strings in the frozen nTLDData inventory. In internet.Best's applicant ranking, the five largest portfolios account for 491 applications, 30.4% of its 1,614-record base. Contention is not an awarded TLD, and a filed application is not a revenue forecast.

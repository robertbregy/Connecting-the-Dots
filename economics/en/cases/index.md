## The economics of the dot

What is a dot worth?

TLDs are naming infrastructure, but they can also create public revenue, recurring registry income, scarcity rents, acquisition value and financial barriers to entry. These cases keep unlike metrics separate rather than forcing them into one league table.

Selection rule: each case illustrates a distinct economic mechanism and is supported by attributable financial or institutional evidence. Revenue, valuation, auction price and public receipts are not interchangeable measures.

## Where does the value come from?

Start with the mechanisms: not everyone invests in an extension for the same reason.

### Eight ways money enters the namespace

The same DNS root can generate very different economic relationships.

01

#### Sovereign digital rent

A country-code namespace becomes a material source of government revenue because global demand attaches value to its two-letter code.

02

#### National contract revenue

A state monetizes its ccTLD through a commercial registry or marketing agreement rather than operating a mass-market registrar business itself.

03

#### Operator acquisition

The company operating one or more namespaces is bought, turning expected registry cash flows into a transaction value.

04

#### Recurring registry economics

Large installed bases and annual renewals can create highly recurring infrastructure revenue, but company-level results must not be mislabeled as revenue from one TLD.

05

#### Valuation & regulation

A registry contract can underpin billion-dollar valuation while price controls, public-interest duties and change-of-control review shape the asset.

06

#### Scarcity & auction

When multiple applicants want the same string, scarcity can be converted into an auction price before the registry has sold a single domain.

07 2012 ROUND · HISTORICAL · PROHIBITED IN 2026

#### Private contention settlement (2012 · historical)

Historical 2012-round mechanism: applicants could receive consideration for withdrawing from contention, so abandoning a string could generate accounting gains. Private contention resolution, including settlement agreements and compensation for withdrawal, is prohibited in the 2026 round.

08

#### Cost of entry

Application fees and additional evaluation costs determine who can afford to compete for a place in the root.

Verisign reported US$1.66 billion of company revenue and US$1.12 billion of operating income in 2025; .com and .net ended the year with 173.5 million registrations combined.

Recurring renewals, scale and high switching friction make mature registry infrastructure economically unlike a normal one-off domain sale. The figures are Verisign company results, not .com-only revenue.

.org

 2019–2024

Valuation & regulation

Core economic question

#### Can a registry contract underpin a billion-dollar valuation?

FACT

The proposed 2019 acquisition of Public Interest Registry was publicly priced at US$1.135 billion, financed in part by a US$360 million term loan facility; ICANN ultimately withheld consent.

**US$1.135B** Proposed PIR purchase price 2019 · USD

.org exposes the tension between financial value and stewardship: an economically valuable recurring namespace can still be constrained by contractual approval, regulation and public-interest expectations.

 2015–2016

Scarcity & auction

Core economic question

#### How much will applicants pay for scarcity before launch?

FACT

ICANN last-resort auctions produced winning prices of US$135 million for .web, US$41.501 million for .shop and US$25.001 million for .app.

**US$135.0M** .web auction price 2016-07-27 · USD

**US$41.501M** .shop auction price 2016-01-27 · USD

**US$25.001M** .app auction price 2015-02-25 · USD

**US$240.59M** Cumulative gross ICANN auction proceeds 2016 · USD

READING

A string can acquire a nine-figure clearing price before operating revenue exists. The price reflects contention scarcity and strategic expectations, not an appraisal of the TLD as property.

Program-level case

 2012–2026

Cost of entry

Core economic question

#### How expensive is the right to compete for the root?

FACT

The base evaluation fee was US$185,000 in the 2012 round and is US$227,000 per application in the 2026 round, before certain additional fees or support discounts.

**US$185,000** Base application evaluation fee 2012 · USD

**US$227,000** Base application evaluation fee 2026 · USD

READING

The application fee is not a purchase price for a TLD. It is an entry cost into an uncertain evaluation process, and therefore part of the economic barrier to participation.

[ICANN — 2012 New gTLD evaluation fee ↗](https://newgtlds.icann.org/en/applicants/global-support/faqs/faqs-en) [ICANN — 2026 gTLD evaluation fee ↗](https://newgtldprogram.icann.org/en/application-rounds/round2/2026-round-general/gtld-evaluation-fee/faqs) [ICANN — 2026 financial projection templates ↗](https://newgtldprogram-2026-agb.icann.org/en/16-appendix-5-templates-for-standard-financial-profile.html)

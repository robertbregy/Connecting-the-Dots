Reader study · facilitator guide

An approximately 15-minute session to learn what sparks curiosity, what is understood and where readers lose the thread. The website is being tested, not the person.

Before the session

  1. Ask for consent to observation and explain that participants may stop at any time. Do not request names, contacts or other identifiers; use codes such as P01. Do not record audio or video without separate consent.
  2. Open the site in the chosen language and record its version and the device. Keep notes outside the website: this page neither collects nor submits answers. The downloadable CSV is empty.
  3. Say: “We are trying out the website. There are no answers to guess. Tell me what you notice and what you are looking for; knowing what is unclear helps us.” Avoid explanations and hints while the person searches.

One session, four moments

  1. 1 minute · first impression

    Show the home page. Ask: “What do you think this is about? What would you open first?” Record the words used before pointing out the short guide.

  2. About 3 minutes · free reading

    Invite the person to use “Start here”. Let them read at their own pace. Note skipped passages, unclear terms and spontaneous interest; time alone does not establish comprehension.

  3. 2 minutes · recall

    Ask the person to look away without rereading: “What would you tell a friend? What surprised you? What remained unclear?” Record the answer without immediately correcting it.

  4. About 9 minutes · finding and discussing

    Reopen the website and use the tasks below without telling the person where to click. Ask what led them to each answer. End with: “What would you remove, add or change?”

Neutral tasks

For non-specialists, use tasks 2–5 after the first impression; add task 6 for researchers. If time runs out, record what was not observed.

  1. What is this site about? What interests you?
  2. Find .ai and explain what it represents.
  3. What does a ranking value count, and when was it observed?
  4. What is .lugano today? What stages remain?
  5. Find the cost of an application and explain what that payment provides.
  6. For researchers: find the source, method, limitations and citation for a current result.

For facilitators: what to look for
  • Observe how the person distinguishes a name, a website and visits; .ai’s origin and its AI association; an application and .lugano’s availability. For rankings, ask what a value counts and its date. Do not reveal these criteria before the answers.
  • Assess use of sources, not recall of a formula. For researchers, also observe whether they distinguish the DOI archive from the current website and can locate limitations.
  • Correct misunderstandings that lead to a false factual conclusion; prioritise those repeated by different people. Record contrary observations too. After changes, repeat affected tasks with new people. Do not publish comprehension percentages or validation results before real observations exist.
